Durability
==========

Durability is the guarantee that data you have saved stays saved. BizKitHub stores every file in its **global distributed storage**, designed for an annual durability of **99.999999999 %** (eleven nines), and keeps an additional copy of every file and every backup in **a second, geographically separated datacentre in Nuremberg, Germany**, operated by a different provider. Database records are protected by mirrored disks, continuous change archiving and write-once backups kept with two independent providers.

This article explains what the durability figure means, which copies exist, which failure each copy protects against, and how durability differs from availability. It is written for system administrators, developers and anyone who has to assess the platform for their own risk analysis.

## What eleven nines mean

The annual durability of 99.999999999 % is the probability that a stored file survives a year without being lost. The remaining 0.000000001 % is the expected yearly loss rate per file.

A practical example: if you store **10 million files**, the statistical expectation is to lose **one file every 10,000 years**. The calculation is simple: 10,000,000 files × 0.00000000001 = 0.0001 files per year, which is one file in 10,000 years.

The figure describes the global distributed storage on its own. The copy in the second datacentre is an additional, independent layer on top of it. Data durability of eleven nines per year is also a headline commitment of the [SLA](/sla-overview).

### Global distributed storage

Every file uploaded in the administration, generated by the platform or received from outside is written to the global distributed storage. The storage keeps redundant copies of each object on several devices. A file counts as uploaded only after it has been stored durably, so a successful upload in the administration or the API means the file is already protected.

### The second datacentre

After a file is stored, the platform copies it automatically to a second datacentre in **Nuremberg, Germany**. It is run by a different provider, in a different location and with separate credentials. A failure, an incident or an operator error at one provider therefore cannot affect the copy at the other.

The copy is made for **every** file of the platform, not only for backups. Large files are copied part by part, and an interrupted copy resumes from the last finished part instead of starting again. The file detail in the administration shows whether the copy in the second datacentre exists. A platform check runs every day and alerts the operations team when any file has waited for its second copy for more than 24 hours.

Backups sent to the [backup service](/backup-service) have a stricter deadline: their copy is due within 1 hour after the upload, plus 1 hour per 8 GiB of size. While the copy is made, the platform checks every part and computes the SHA-256 checksum of the whole file, so each backup shows both whether its copy arrived on time and whether its content matches the checksum your client sent.

### Immutable storage

Backups are stored **write-once**. Backups sent to the backup service cannot be deleted or changed by anyone, not even by a BizKitHub administrator. Backups of the platform database cannot be changed or deleted before their retention period ends, not even with administrator credentials. A compromised server, a stolen password or a mistaken command has nothing to erase.

## Durability and availability

Durability and availability are two different guarantees:

- **Durability** says that the data is not lost. It is protected by the copies described above.
- **Availability** says that you can reach the service at a given moment. It is measured as the monthly uptime of the API and the administration.

The [SLA](/sla-overview) for the Pro tier guarantees a monthly uptime of **99.90 %**. The API and the administration each run as two independent instances behind a load balancer, and updates are rolled out one instance at a time without a maintenance window. See [Infrastructure](/infrastructure-overview).

An outage reduces availability but not durability. If a datacentre becomes unreachable, the data stays safe in the other copies, and the service is restored from them. Restoring the platform from backups takes hours rather than days. The progress of any incident is published on the [status page](https://status.bizkithub.com).

Backups in the backup service stay available even when their primary copy cannot be served: a download in the administration is then served from the copy in the second datacentre.

## Recovery point and recovery time

- **Recovery point.** Because the database can be restored to any moment in the archive, the maximum data loss after a total loss of the primary server is 15 minutes of changes. Failures smaller than a server, such as a single disk, lose nothing. Stored files lose nothing in any of the scenarios above.
- **Recovery time.** Restoring the platform from backups takes hours rather than days.

## What durability does not cover

Durability protects data that the platform has accepted. It does not undo changes that were made intentionally through the administration or the API: an order you cancelled stays cancelled, and a file you deleted is removed from your organisation. For accidental changes, see the recovery section in [Backup and recovery](/backup-overview).

Durability also does not replace your own backups of systems outside BizKitHub. Databases and files on your own servers are protected only if you back them up, for example with the [backup service](/backup-service).
